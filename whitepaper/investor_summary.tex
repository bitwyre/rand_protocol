\section{Executive Summary}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{Rand is a privacy layer for Solana --- not a new blockchain.}

Solana settles more stablecoin volume than any other high-performance chain, and native USDT and USDC are issued directly on it. Everything is also completely public: every balance, every counterparty, every amount. That rules Solana out for payroll, treasury operations, supplier payments, and trading --- any activity a business cannot publish.

Rand fixes exactly that, and nothing else:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Private transfers of the stablecoins people already hold.} Deposit native USDT, transact confidentially, withdraw. No bridge, no wrapped assets, no new chain to bootstrap.
    \item \textbf{Deployed as Solana programs.} We inherit Solana's speed, finality, liquidity, and validator set instead of rebuilding them. No validators to recruit, no security budget to fund, no chicken-and-egg liquidity problem.
    \item \textbf{Post-quantum confidentiality.} Encrypted transfer data on a public ledger is permanently exposed to future quantum decryption. Rand encrypts with the NIST post-quantum standards from day one.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{
\textbf{Why this is the right structure:} a privacy Layer 1 must attract its own liquidity, its own validators, and its own stablecoin issuers before it is useful. Rand starts with all three, because they are already on Solana.
}}
\end{center}

\section{The Problem}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Problem 1: Solana Has No Privacy}

Imagine if every bank transfer your company made was permanently visible to competitors, suppliers, and employees. That is Solana today. Balances, counterparties, and amounts are public by default.

Existing options all fail for a business holding USDT on Solana:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Zcash, Monero:} strong privacy, but separate networks. Your USDT is not there, and getting it there means bridging.
    \item \textbf{Token-2022 confidential transfers:} hides amounts but not who pays whom --- and cannot be enabled on the existing USDT and USDC mints, because the feature must be set when a token is created.
    \item \textbf{Privacy rollups:} own gas token and own liquidity, so assets must be bridged and liquidity fragmented.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Problem 2: Privacy Solutions Force You Off-Chain}

Every option above asks a business to move assets somewhere else. Bridges are the single largest category of loss in crypto history --- Ronin \$624M, Poly \$611M, BNB Bridge \$570M, Wormhole \$325M. Asking a treasurer to bridge their stablecoins to obtain privacy is asking them to accept a worse risk than the one they are solving.

\subsection{Problem 3: Tomorrow's Quantum Computers Read Today's Ledger}

Encrypted data published to a blockchain stays there permanently. An adversary can record it now and decrypt it once quantum computers mature --- "harvest now, decrypt later." Privacy systems using conventional encryption are therefore not private in the long run; they are merely delayed.

\section{Rand's Solution}
%==============================================================================

Rand adds three Solana programs and one off-chain role. That is the whole system.

\subsection{The Shielded Pool}

\textbf{What it does:} holds deposited USDT, USDC, and SOL, and tracks who owns what without revealing it.

Deposits are visible. Once inside, transfers are not: sender, recipient, and amount are hidden behind a zero-knowledge proof. Withdrawals are visible again. All assets are the real, native tokens throughout --- never a wrapped derivative.

\subsection{The Relayers (Earn SHRUGG)}

\textbf{What they do:} submit your private transaction and pay the Solana network fee on your behalf.

This role exists for a precise reason. Solana charges fees in SOL, and every transaction names who paid. If you pay your own fee, you have signed your name to your "private" transfer and defeated the entire system. So somebody else must submit it --- and be paid without creating a second trail. That payment is SHRUGG, deducted inside the zero-knowledge proof.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Requirement:} an account with SOL. No stake, no permission, no special hardware.
    \item \textbf{Payment:} SHRUGG, from every transfer they submit
    \item \textbf{Competition:} relayers are interchangeable and cannot alter what they submit --- only submit it or decline
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Why SHRUGG has durable demand:} it is not a reward paid out of inflation. Every confidential transfer must buy it to pay a relayer, so demand scales with usage rather than with emissions.

\subsection{Solana Itself}

\textbf{What it provides:} ordering, finality, security, and liquidity --- for free, from our perspective.

Solana validators run unmodified Agave or Firedancer and need not know Rand exists. We are a program on their chain, not a competitor for their stake.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Speed:} Solana's, not a number we have to prove
    \item \textbf{Security:} secured by the full value staked on Solana
    \item \textbf{Assets:} native USDT and USDC, already there
\end{itemize}

\section{A Single Token}
%==============================================================================

Rand has one token. Earlier designs had two --- ATLAS for staking and gas, SHRUGG for privacy --- but once Solana provides consensus there is no validator set for a staking token to secure and no separate gas to price. \textbf{SOL fills that role, so ATLAS was removed.}

\subsection{SHRUGG --- The Confidential Transfer Token}

\textbf{Purpose:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Pay relayers to submit confidential transfers
    \item The only way to pay for privacy without revealing who you are
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Supply:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Initial: 200 million tokens
    \item Emission: 6\% declining to 1\%, on a fixed published schedule
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Why emission is fixed rather than demand-linked:} tying issuance to shielded activity would let anyone read private transaction volume off the supply curve. We will not build a side channel into monetary policy.

\subsection{Why One Token Is Stronger}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{No inflation subsidy.} SHRUGG demand comes from users buying it to transact, not from rewards printed to attract validators.
    \item \textbf{Structural necessity.} Every confidential transfer requires SHRUGG. It is not an optional governance token bolted onto a protocol that works without it.
    \item \textbf{Nothing to bootstrap.} A staking token needs a validator set, and a validator set needs a token price. We skip that loop entirely.
\end{enumerate}

\section{zUSDT: Private Stablecoin, No Peg Risk}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{The Terra Lesson}

Terra's UST collapsed in May 2022 --- \$60B destroyed, LUNA supply inflated 18{,}500$\times$. The cause was structural: UST's peg was held by a mechanism rather than by reserves, and a mechanism can be attacked.

\subsection{zUSDT's Design: No Mechanism At All}

zUSDT is fully reserved. Every unit is backed by one unit of native USDT held in a Solana program, redeemable on demand.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Backing:} 100\% native USDT. Not 90\%, not fractional, not partly in our own token.
    \item \textbf{Reserve location:} the same chain as the token --- no bridge between the two
    \item \textbf{Verification:} anyone can compare reserves to supply on-chain, continuously
    \item \textbf{Privacy:} transacts confidentially through the Rand pool
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What it does not have, deliberately:} no collateral ratio to manage, no absorber token to dilute, no redemption rate limits, no monetary policy. A fully-reserved token cannot enter a death spiral, because there is no reflexive loop to spiral.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.9\textwidth}{
\textbf{We abandoned a more ambitious design to get here.} An earlier proposal, USDB, was fractionally backed with SOL and SHRUGG absorbing volatility, defended by dynamic collateral ratios and rate-limited redemptions. It was a sound design. But every one of those defences exists to manage the possibility of backing falling short --- and full reserving removes that possibility outright. We chose fewer moving parts.
}}
\end{center}

\subsection{The Risk We Cannot Remove}

Tether can freeze USDT balances, and has done so at law-enforcement request. If our reserve were frozen, redemption would halt. No design that wraps a centrally-administered asset can prevent this, and we would rather state it plainly than bury it. zUSDT offers privacy from observers, not from the issuer. Mitigation is to shard the reserve across vaults so a single freeze impairs only part of redemption capacity.

\section{Post-Quantum Security}
%==============================================================================

Encrypted data on a public ledger is permanent. An adversary can record it today and decrypt it whenever quantum computers mature. Rand therefore uses the NIST post-quantum standards where it matters most --- for confidentiality.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Component} & \textbf{Typical} & \textbf{Rand} & \textbf{Quantum-safe?} \\ \midrule
Transfer data encryption & ECDH & ML-KEM (Kyber) & \textbf{Yes} \\
Spend authorisation & ECDSA & ML-DSA (Dilithium) & \textbf{Yes} \\
Commitments & Pedersen & Poseidon & \textbf{Yes} \\
Hashing & SHA-256 & SHA-3, BLAKE3 & \textbf{Yes} \\
Proof verification & --- & Groth16 & \textbf{No --- see below} \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{We are explicit about the exception.} Verifying proofs on Solana requires pairing arithmetic that is not quantum-safe. This is a deliberate trade, not an oversight, and the reasoning is that the two risks are not symmetric:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Confidentiality cannot be fixed later.} A ciphertext published today is decryptable forever once the maths breaks. So it must be post-quantum now.
    \item \textbf{Verification can be fixed later.} It is enforced by a program we can upgrade. Replace the verifier before quantum computers arrive and nothing historical is at risk.
\end{itemize}

Spending the post-quantum budget on the irreversible risk is the correct allocation. The residual exposure is that a quantum adversary, acting before we upgrade, could forge a proof and steal from the pool --- bounded, detectable through reserve auditing, and mitigated by upgrading. They could not decrypt anyone's transaction history. Competitors claiming end-to-end post-quantum security on Solana today are either not verifying proofs on-chain or not being precise.

\subsection{Risk Factors \& Mitigations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Program defect:} the pool is a concentrated, publicly valued target. Mitigation: deposit caps, two independent audits, formal verification of mint/redeem and state transitions.
    \item \textbf{Anonymity set size:} privacy is weakest at launch, exactly when users may assume otherwise. Mitigation: communicate this plainly; a single multi-asset pool rather than one per asset.
    \item \textbf{Solana dependency:} our safety ceiling is Solana's. A consensus failure there could permit a double-spend here. Accepted deliberately in exchange for native assets and no security budget.
    \item \textbf{Tether freeze:} could halt zUSDT redemption. Mitigation: reserve sharding. Cannot be eliminated.
    \item \textbf{Quantum forgery before verifier upgrade:} bounded by pool size, detectable via reserve auditing, mitigated by upgrading the verifier.
    \item \textbf{Regulatory:} privacy tooling attracts scrutiny. Mitigation: no protocol-level anonymity for the issuer, optional viewing keys for holders needing to disclose to auditors or regulators, KYC at fiat on/off-ramps.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why Rand Will Win}
%==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{We start where the liquidity is.} Competitors must attract stablecoin issuers, validators, and liquidity to a new chain before being useful. Native USDT and USDC are already on Solana.
    \item \textbf{No bridge.} The largest losses in crypto are bridge failures. Reserve and token share a chain, so there is no bridge to attack.
    \item \textbf{Structural token demand.} Every confidential transfer must buy SHRUGG to pay a relayer. Demand tracks usage, not emissions.
    \item \textbf{Honest post-quantum positioning.} We are post-quantum where it is irreversible and explicit about where we are not. That distinction is defensible under scrutiny; blanket claims are not.
    \item \textbf{Small surface.} Three programs and a fee token. No consensus protocol, no validator set, no staking, no stablecoin peg mechanism --- each an entire class of failure we do not carry.
\end{enumerate}
